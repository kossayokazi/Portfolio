Starting out as a freelancer while still early in my career taught me things no course really prepares you for. Here's what stuck with me.

## Clients don't buy code, they buy outcomes

Nobody hires a developer because they want React components. They hire you because they want more bookings, fewer support emails, or a product they can finally show investors. Framing every conversation around the outcome, not the tech, changes how clients see you.

## Scope creep is a communication problem, not a client problem

Early on, I said yes to "just one more small thing" too often. The fix wasn't refusing requests, it was writing a clear scope upfront and treating changes as a normal, expected part of the process, with a quick conversation about time and cost before starting them.

## Undercharging doesn't win loyalty

I used to think a lower price would make clients happier. It didn't. Clients who negotiate hardest on price are often the ones who respect your time the least afterward. Charging fairly, and explaining your reasoning, tends to attract better clients overall.

## Documentation is part of the deliverable

A project isn't done when the code works, it's done when the client (or the next developer) can understand it without you. I now treat a short README and a handover note as part of every project, not an afterthought.

## Keep shipping in public

Every project I've documented publicly, on GitHub or in write-ups like this one, has led to a conversation I didn't expect. You don't need a huge audience for this to work, you just need to be visible and consistent.
